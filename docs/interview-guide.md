# Interview Guide

## Conducting Effective System Interviews

---

## Before the Interview

### 1. Preparation Checklist

- [ ] Review interviewee's role and department
- [ ] Understand their relationship with the system
- [ ] Review any previous feedback from this person/role
- [ ] Prepare customized questions if needed
- [ ] Test recording equipment (if using)
- [ ] Confirm meeting details sent

### 2. Materials to Have Ready

- Interview questions document
- Note-taking template
- Consent form (if required)
- Recording device (if permitted)
- Pen and paper as backup

### 3. Environment Setup

- Quiet, private space
- Minimize interruptions
- Allow for overrun time
- Water available

---

## During the Interview

### 1. Opening (5 minutes)

**Script:**
> "Thank you for taking the time to speak with me today. I'm [Name], and I'm conducting interviews as part of our systems analysis project. The goal is to understand how you and your colleagues use [System Name] and identify opportunities for improvement.

> This interview will take approximately [X] minutes. I'll be taking notes, and with your permission, I'd like to record this conversation to ensure accuracy. Your responses will be kept confidential, and any quotes used in our report will be anonymized.

> Do you have any questions before we begin?"

### 2. Building Rapport

- Start with easy, open-ended questions
- Show genuine interest in their work
- Use active listening
- Acknowledge their expertise

### 3. Asking Questions

**Do:**
- Ask one question at a time
- Use open-ended questions
- Allow silence for thinking
- Follow up on interesting points
- Ask for specific examples

**Don't:**
- Lead the witness ("So you find it frustrating, right?")
- Interrupt
- Rush through questions
- Show judgment or bias
- Fill silences too quickly

### 4. Probing Techniques

| Technique | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| Clarification | "Can you tell me more about that?" |
| Example | "Can you give me a specific example?" |
| Contrast | "How does this compare to [X]?" |
| Impact | "How does this affect your work?" |
| Frequency | "How often does this happen?" |
| The 5 Whys | "Why is that important?" (repeat) |

### 5. Handling Difficult Situations

**Interviewee goes off-topic:**
> "That's interesting. Let me note that, and we can come back to it if we have time. For now, I'd like to focus on..."

**Interviewee is very negative:**
> "I understand there are challenges. To help us prioritize, which issue impacts your work the most?"

**Interviewee gives short answers:**
> "Can you walk me through what that looks like day-to-day?"

**Interviewee doesn't use the system much:**
> "Even occasional usage is valuable. When you do use it, what's that experience like?"

### 6. Closing (5 minutes)

**Script:**
> "Thank you for sharing your insights. Before we finish:

> - Is there anything else about [System Name] you'd like to share?
> - Is there anyone else you'd recommend I speak with?
> - Can I follow up if I have clarifying questions?

> Thank you again for your time. Your input is very valuable for this project."

## After the Interview

### 1. Immediate Actions (Within 30 minutes)

- [ ] Review and clean up notes
- [ ] Highlight key quotes
- [ ] Note any follow-up questions
- [ ] Record initial impressions
- [ ] Save/backup recording

### 2. Documentation (Within 24 hours)

- [ ] Complete interview notes template
- [ ] Transcribe key sections (if needed)
- [ ] Add to interview tracking log
- [ ] Identify emerging themes

### 3. Follow-up

- Send thank you email
- Clarify any ambiguous points
- Share any promised materials

---

## Interview Note-Taking Tips

### What to Capture

- Verbatim quotes (mark with quotation marks)
- Specific examples and stories
- Emotional reactions (frustration, enthusiasm)
- Frequency and impact of issues
- Workarounds they've developed
- Feature requests and wishes

### Notation System

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| "..." | Verbatim quote |
| !! | Strong emotion |
| ? | Need to clarify |
| → | Follow-up needed |
| * | Key insight |
| PP | Pain point |
| + | Positive/strength |

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Talking too much** - Aim for 80% listening, 20% talking
2. **Accepting vague answers** - Always probe for specifics
3. **Missing the emotion** - Note frustration, enthusiasm, resignation
4. **Forgetting context** - Ask about frequency, impact, duration
5. **Leading questions** - Stay neutral and curious
6. **Poor time management** - Watch the clock, prioritize questions
7. **Incomplete notes** - When in doubt, write it down

---

## Quality Checklist

After each interview, verify:

- [ ] All required questions were asked
- [ ] Specific examples were captured
- [ ] Key quotes were recorded verbatim
- [ ] Follow-up items were identified
- [ ] Interviewee sentiment was noted
- [ ] Notes are complete and readable
