# Flexite Interview Questions - Rationale Guide (Streamlined)
## Table Format - 31 Strategic Questions

---

## Section 1: Opening & Overview

| Question | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **How long have you been the super user for Flexite?** | Establishes Jan-Eric's credibility and experience depth. Long tenure (3+ years) = Deep understanding of incident patterns, system evolution, safety culture development. Can compare "before and after" Flexite. Newer (<1 year) = Fresh perspective but limited historical context. High turnover suggests system problems or H&S not valued. Key person dependency if Jan-Eric is sole expert on system and safety processes. |
| **What's your role in relation to health and safety management?** | Reveals if H&S is strategic (dedicated) or tactical (add-on). Dedicated H&S Manager = Proper resources, time for proactive safety, can develop culture. Multiple hats ("H&S plus HR, facilities, office management") = Stretched thin, reactive only, prevention neglected. Organizational signal: Dedicated role = Safety valued. Add-on = Compliance checkbox. If overwhelmed, Flexite won't be used optimally regardless of capabilities. |
| **What types of incidents does Flexite handle?** | Reveals safety culture maturity. Level 1 (injuries only) = Immature, reactive, compliance-focused, missing 99% of prevention opportunities. Level 2 (all injuries) = Developing but still reactive. Level 3 (injuries + near-misses) = Maturing, learning from close calls. Level 4 (comprehensive: injuries, near-misses, property damage, unsafe conditions) = **MATURE**, proactive prevention. Heinrich's Pyramid: 1 serious injury = 10 minor injuries = 30 property damage = 600 near-misses = 1,000 unsafe acts. Only logging injuries = Missing prevention opportunities. |
| **How many incident reports per month through Flexite?** | Volume reveals reporting culture and safety performance. Very low (<2-3/month for medium facility) = Either excellent safety (good) OR severe underreporting (bad). Moderate (5-15/month) = Typical decent reporting. High (20+/month) = Excellent reporting culture capturing near-misses (good) OR serious safety problems (bad - need context). Benchmarks: Mature culture = 5-10 reports per 100 employees/month. If very low, critical question: "How do you know this represents all incidents?" Low volume + known incidents = Underreporting crisis. |

## Section 2: System Value & Criticality

| Question | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **What if Flexite unavailable for a week? How would incidents be reported?** | Tests true criticality and business continuity. "Serious compliance trouble - legally required" = Mission critical, Arbetsmiljöverket requires reporting. "Use paper forms then enter later" = Good continuity planning (positive sign). "Probably not track for a week" = **RED FLAG**, compliance violation, lost data, liability exposure. Legal context: Serious accidents require 24-hour reporting to Arbetsmiljöverket. Missing reports = Fines 50,000-500,000 SEK. Good backup process should include: pre-printed forms, clear procedures, secure temporary storage, data entry plan, tested annually. |
| **On scale 1-10, how critical for H&S compliance? Why?** | Should be 8-10 for incident management. Rating 9-10 with clear reasoning ("legally required, can't demonstrate due diligence without it") = Credible, justified investment. Rating 5-6 = **YELLOW FLAG**, should not be this low. Rating 3-4 = **RED FLAG**, system failed or regulatory requirements not understood. The "why" matters more than number. Strong answer includes: specific legal requirements, Arbetsmiljöverket mentions, audit trail needs, compliance demonstrations. Weak: "We use it every day" (frequency ≠ criticality). |
| **Does Flexite help meet legal reporting requirements?** | **MOST CRITICAL QUESTION for Swedish incident management.** Arbetsmiljöverket requires: Immediate reporting (24 hours) for serious accidents, documentation of all work injuries, investigation and root cause, corrective actions tracked, available for inspection. "Yes, absolutely - ensures all Arbetsmiljöverket requirements" = **GREEN FLAG**, core value delivered. "Helps with most, supplement manually" = **YELLOW FLAG**, gaps exist. "Not sure" = **RED ALERT**, serious compliance risk. "No" = **CRITICAL FAILURE**. Non-compliance costs: fines 50,000-500,000 SEK, increased inspections, stop-work orders, reputational damage. This alone can justify or disqualify entire investment. |
| **Who benefits most from Flexite?** | Identifies stakeholder value distribution. Safety team only = Weak business case. Management (visibility into risks) = Strategic value. Employees (safer workplace) = Cultural value. Insurance (reduced premiums) = Measurable ROI. Compliance (avoid fines) = Risk mitigation. Broad benefits = Stronger investment justification. If Jan-Eric struggles to articulate benefits beyond "we need it for compliance" = Limited value perception. |

## Section 3: Usage & Adoption

| Question | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **How frequently do you use Flexite?** | Frequency indicates incident volume and Jan-Eric's workload. Daily use = Either high incident volume (concerning) or active management (good - need context). Weekly = Moderate volume or effective processes. Monthly = Very low incidents (great if true) or underutilization (concerning). For H&S Manager, usage frequency should match incident reality. If incidents happen daily but Jan-Eric uses monthly = Major gap, work happening off-system. |
| **Who can report incidents in Flexite?** | Accessibility determines reporting effectiveness. **BEST PRACTICE: All employees** = Democratized safety, maximum visibility. Managers only = Filtered reporting, miss frontline incidents. Safety team only = **RED FLAG**, major underreporting, incidents filtered before logging. Restricted access indicates: system design flaw (too complicated for general use), intentional underreporting (keep numbers low), or cultural issues (don't trust employees). If not everyone can report, you're missing incidents and early warnings. |
| **Do people use Flexite or still call/email you?** | **THE CRITICAL ADOPTION QUESTION.** If people bypass Flexite = System has **FAILED**. Phone/email creates: no documentation trail (regulatory risk), delayed response, can't analyze patterns, no audit trail, Sofia becomes bottleneck, incidents forgotten. "Mostly call me" = **MAJOR RED FLAG**, system too complicated, not user-friendly, or cultural resistance. No mobile access often causes this. Impact: Missing incidents, can't demonstrate compliance, can't prevent recurrence. This is #1 incident system implementation failure. If bypass rate >30% = Crisis requiring immediate attention. |
| **What % of incidents properly reported through system?** | Measures true reporting culture and system effectiveness. **BENCHMARK: Should be 95%+.** If 60% = 40% of incidents invisible (major blind spots, can't identify patterns, regulatory non-compliance, liability exposure). Underreporting causes: Fear of blame ("snitching culture"), system too complicated, no perceived value, management doesn't act on reports, pressure to keep numbers low. Low reporting = Safety management illusion (think you're managing but actually blind). Calculate: If 200 employees × 5% incident rate = 10 incidents/year expected, but only 5 reported = Missing 50% of incidents. |
| **Are there incidents that should be reported but aren't?** | Reveals coverage gaps and barriers. Common unreported: Near-misses ("nothing happened so why report"), minor injuries ("just a scratch"), unsafe conditions ("someone else's problem"), contractor incidents ("not our employees"). If yes = Serious gaps, need process/training/cultural change. Each unreported incident = Lost prevention opportunity. Near-misses especially critical - they're early warning system for serious incidents. |

## Section 4: User Experience

| Question | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **What do people like most about Flexite?** | Identifies strengths to preserve in any change. Common appreciations: Easy mobile reporting, automatic notifications, good tracking, management visibility. These = NON-NEGOTIABLE in replacement. If Jan-Eric struggles to name positives = Poor user satisfaction, wrong system fit. Strong specific answer = Value delivered. What users value might differ from management assumptions. |
| **Top 3 frustrations with Flexite?** | Forces prioritization. Common frustrations: Poor mobile experience, too many required fields, slow system, difficult to find past incidents, unclear workflow, inadequate notifications. These become: vendor negotiation points, replacement criteria, training focus, quick fix opportunities. Quantify impact where possible: "Complicated form means people don't report minor incidents" = Lost prevention opportunities. Never-fixed frustrations = Vendor neglect or fundamental design issues. |
| **How easy to report an incident?** | **FRICTION KILLS REPORTING.** Best practice: Mobile app or QR code on equipment, maximum 5 required fields, can attach photo immediately, <2 minutes to complete, available 24/7. If reporting is hard (needs computer, many fields, complicated workflow) = People won't report minor incidents, near-misses go unreported, miss prevention opportunities. Hard reporting especially impacts: frontline workers (no desk access), shift workers (off-hours), contractors (not trained on system). "Very easy" with mobile = High adoption likely. "Difficult" = Adoption failure. |
| **Do responsible persons respond promptly?** | Tests action management and accountability. Reporting is pointless without action. Prompt response: Creates trust in system (encourages reporting), prevents recurrence, shows commitment to safety. Delayed response: People stop reporting ("nothing happens anyway"), incidents recur, system loses credibility, culture deteriorates. If "rarely" or "sometimes" = **RED FLAG**, broken accountability, need process fix not system fix. Should have: automatic notifications, due dates, escalation for overdue, management visibility into open actions. |
| **Can you easily track corrective actions completion?** | Accountability and closure tracking. Incidents shouldn't just be reported - must lead to actions tracked to completion. Can't track = Managing safety in the dark, don't know if problems are fixed, recurrence likely. Good tracking shows: What was done, who did it, when completed, verification, effectiveness. This is key for: demonstrating continuous improvement, regulatory compliance, preventing recurrence, management visibility. If difficult = Process gap or system limitation requiring fix. |

## Section 5: IT Support & System Performance

| Question | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **How often engage IT for Flexite issues?** | IT support frequency indicates system stability. Daily = Major problems. Monthly = Typical. Rarely = Very stable or Jan-Eric stopped reporting (concerning). Hidden cost calculation: 5 IT hours/month × 800 SEK × 12 = 48,000 SEK/year beyond license. High support suggests: unstable system, poor usability, inadequate training, or system too complex for needs. |
| **Issues with mobile access?** | **MOBILE IS CRITICAL for incident reporting.** People need to report from incident location with photos, real-time reporting, no need for computer access. If mobile doesn't work well = Delayed reporting (details forgotten), incomplete documentation (no photos), lower adoption (wait until at desk), might not report at all. Poor mobile access is TOP reason for incident system adoption failure. Modern systems must be mobile-first. If "major issues" or "no mobile" = **RED FLAG**, serious adoption barrier. |
| **Is system performance acceptable?** | Performance affects adoption. Fast system = No friction, people use it. Slow system (>30 seconds to load) = People give up, report via email/phone instead, frustration, lower adoption. Especially critical for mobile - if app is slow on production floor, won't be used. "Poor" performance = Hidden adoption killer. Even with good features, poor performance destroys value. |

## Section 6: Integration & Effectiveness

| Question | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **Does Flexite integrate with other systems?** | Integration increases value and efficiency. HR integration = Track injury rates by department, employee records linked. Facilities integration = Coordinate repairs for unsafe conditions. Email notifications = Automatic alerts to responsible parties. No integration = Manual work (Jan-Eric emails people, copies data elsewhere), information silos, delays. Good integration multiplies Flexite value. If no integrations = Isolated tool, limited value, high manual effort. |
| **Automatic notifications to responsible persons?** | **AUTOMATION DRIVES ACCOUNTABILITY.** Automatic = Immediate awareness, no delay, Jan-Eric doesn't chase people, creates urgency, ensures action. Manual = Jan-Eric must email/call people, delays response, things fall through cracks, becomes bottleneck. If "no" or "sometimes" = Major efficiency loss, accountability problem. Calculate: If Jan-Eric manually notifies 10 people/month × 15 min = 30 hours/year = 15,000 SEK wasted on work that should be automated. |
| **Track preventive actions vs. just reactive?** | Measures strategic value vs. tactical logging. **Reactive only** (log incident → investigate → close) = Treats symptoms, incidents recur, limited value. **Preventive** (log → investigate → root cause → preventive actions → track completion) = Prevents recurrence, improves culture, strategic value, demonstrates continuous improvement. If "only reactive" = **YELLOW FLAG**, system being used as logbook only, missing prevention value that could save serious incidents. Mature safety = Preventive focus. |
| **Can you analyze incident trends and patterns?** | **ANALYTICS PREVENT FUTURE INCIDENTS.** Should easily answer: Which departments have most incidents? What times/days are incidents most common? What types recur? Are rates improving? Where should we focus prevention efforts? If can't analyze = **Data exists but not actionable**, reactive only, can't prevent recurrence, missing strategic value. Example: Analysis shows slips/trips increasing in Building A = Focus prevention there. Without analytics, patterns invisible. |

## Section 7: Compliance & Documentation

| Question | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **Adequate audit trails for investigations?** | Legal protection requires complete documentation. Arbetsmiljöverket inspections, legal proceedings, insurance claims all need: complete investigation timeline, actions taken, responsible parties, completion verification, supporting documents. Poor audit trail = Legal exposure, can't demonstrate due diligence, compliance risk, liability. If "no" or "partially" = **RED FLAG**, serious risk requiring immediate attention. Good audit trail shows: who did what when, evidence preserved, investigator notes, approvals, closures. |
| **Demonstrate compliance to auditors using Flexite data?** | **THE ULTIMATE TEST.** During Arbetsmiljöverket inspection, must demonstrate incident management processes. Can Jan-Eric: Pull complete incident history, show investigation timelines, prove corrective actions completed, generate required reports, show trend analysis? If "yes, easily" = **GREEN FLAG**, system fulfilling compliance purpose. If "difficult" or "no" = **RED FLAG**, will fail inspection, compliance violations likely, fines incoming. System's primary purpose = Enable compliance demonstration. If can't do this, system has failed regardless of other features. |

## Section 8: Future Outlook

| Question | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **Is Flexite meeting current H&S reporting needs?** | Overall satisfaction assessment. "Yes, fully" = System working well, investment justified. "Partially" = Gaps exist requiring investigation (which gaps? Can they be fixed?). "No" = Serious problems, system failing, action required. Follow up partial/no with: What specific needs unmet? This reveals: system limitations (might need replacement), configuration problems (can be fixed), process issues (not system's fault), training gaps (need education), or cultural barriers (need change management). |
| **What features missing that would improve incident management?** | Innovation opportunities and competitive analysis. Common missing: Better mobile app, photo/video capability, integration with training systems, predictive analytics, automated risk assessments, easier reporting workflow. These become: vendor negotiation points ("we need feature X or we'll switch"), replacement evaluation criteria, custom development opportunities. If Jan-Eric immediately lists 5+ features = He's researched alternatives, probably frustrated. Can't think of any = Either satisfied or hasn't thought about optimization. |
| **Recommend: invest more, maintain, or explore alternatives?** | **THE ULTIMATE QUESTION.** Jan-Eric's daily experience with incidents makes recommendation invaluable. "Invest more" = System working, expand use. "Maintain" = Acceptable, no changes needed. "Explore alternatives" = Serious problems warrant investigation. Reasoning reveals: satisfaction level, awareness of alternatives, pain point severity, compliance concerns. Management should heavily weight this answer. If H&S Manager wants to replace = Likely good reason. If enthusiastically recommends continuing = Strong validation. |
| **Any other thoughts about Flexite?** | Open-ended catch-all for issues not covered. Often reveals: cultural issues (blame culture preventing reporting), relationship with vendor, regulatory concerns, upcoming changes, politics. Listen for hesitations or lowered voice (sensitive topics). "Between you and me..." often contains most valuable insights. This is where Jan-Eric shares what he didn't want to say on record earlier. Might reveal: management doesn't take safety seriously, incidents being hidden, pressure to keep numbers low. |

## The 5 Make-or-Break Questions

If you only had 20 minutes, ask these:

1. **Q7: Does it meet legal reporting requirements?** (Compliance is non-negotiable)
2. **Q11: Do people use it or call/email?** (Adoption test - if bypass, system failed)
3. **Q12: What % of incidents reported?** (Should be 95%+)
4. **Q24: Track preventive actions?** (Strategic value vs. just logging)
5. **Q27: Can you demonstrate compliance to auditors?** (Ultimate test)

**These 5 give management 80% of decision-making information.**
